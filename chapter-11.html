CHAPTER 11 · ENTRY & STOP-LOSS PLANNING

Types of Trade Execution

U.O.P Theory Bootcamp · Lesson 11 of 12

What you will learn

Resolve a common trading question: “Where should I place my stop-loss?” Compare three entry styles, calculate their risk, and understand the risk-cover checkpoint before aiming for Side Up targets.

  1. Choose the entry condition: direct, reclaim or TCL cross.
  2. Define the stop and calculate the actual entry-to-stop distance.
  3. Plan risk reduction and review Side Up 1 and Side Up 2.
Three entries · three different stop references

Define the entry and the stop together.

Many traders recognise a level but remain unsure where to place the stop-loss. U.O.P separates execution into three styles: buying directly near Participation, buying after Participation is reclaimed, and buying after price crosses TCL. Each style waits for a different event, so the stop reference and actual risk can differ.

The examples below use Participation (P) = 200 and Optimal (O) = 180. They are buy-side premium scenarios for a selected CE or PE. Use the selected option’s own levels and confirmation chart.

01

Direct Participation Entry · The Risky Trade

Enter earlier, before the reclaim is confirmed.

The option premium falls from a higher level toward Participation. In this execution style, the trader buys near 200 on the first touch or direct break, anticipating a rebound. The method’s initial stop distance is one-third of the Participation–Optimal difference, following the worked calculation below.

Initial stop calculation

P − O = 200 − 180 = 20 points

Stop distance = 20 ÷ 3 = 6.67 points, approximately 7 points.

At an exact 200 entry, the nominal initial stop is 200 − 6.67 = 193.33.

A 10–14-point stop corresponds to a P–O width of approximately 30–42 points under this formula. It is not a fixed distance for every strike.

Why is it called risky?

After breaking Participation, the premium may continue lower instead of returning above 200. The stop can be hit directly. The early entry is an anticipation of support, so the rebound has not yet been established.

If a new low forms at 196

The distance from entry 200 to that swing low is 4 points. If a valid swing forms and the written plan permits tightening the stop below it, 204 becomes the simplified four-point reward checkpoint. Do not treat a developing low as confirmed automatically.

Keep the two risk references separate: with the original stop near 193.33, initial risk remains 6.67 points and the initial 1R price is about 206.67. The 204 checkpoint comes from the later 196 swing reference. Tightening the stop changes the remaining risk; it does not rewrite the original entry risk.

02

Participation Reclaim Entry

Let the premium dip, then wait for its return above P.

  1. The option breaks below 200.
  2. It forms an observed swing low at 196.
  3. It crosses back above Participation 200; this reclaim is the method’s buy-activation event.
  4. Place the planned stop below the swing low and calculate risk from the actual fill.
The 200 → 196 → 204 example

Using the swing low as the simplified stop reference: 200 − 196 = 4 points of nominal risk.

At 204, the premium is 4 points above entry: a simplified 1R checkpoint.

For the actual order, a stop below 196 adds a buffer. For example, entry 200 with SL 195.50 gives risk of 4.50 points and a 1R price of 204.50, before fees and execution differences.

At the risk-management checkpoint, apply the planned stop adjustment or partial booking. Then monitor Side Up 1 and Side Up 2 as the next method targets. Their values come from the selected option’s live roadmap. A reclaim can still fail, so keep the defined invalidation.

03

TCL-Confirmed Entry

Use the Trend Confirmation Line shown by your indicator.

TCL means Trend Confirmation Line. In this scenario, TCL initially sits above Participation. The premium breaks below 200 while TCL moves closer to Participation. Instead of entering on the first touch, wait for the premium to recover and cross above TCL.

Where does the stop go?

Below the small swing low near Participation that supports the TCL-cross setup. Record the actual entry and stop: risk = entry − stop. Because the TCL-cross entry may be above 200, do not assume its risk is the same four points as Type 2.

What comes next?

Use actual entry + the defined risk distance for the 1R checkpoint. Apply the planned risk adjustment, then review Side Up 1 and Side Up 2. Later main targets still require the separate confirmation taught in Chapter 8.

You can view the indicator and its TCL through AlphaEdgePro U.O.P Theory V2.1. Review its boards in Chapter 5.

Make the risk-cover step concrete

Reaching 204 is a management checkpoint.

For the simplified 200-entry / 196-stop example, 204 gives a four-point unrealised gain. The price reaching that level alone does not remove the position’s risk. The course’s phrase “risk cover, reward open” needs an actual management action, written before entry.

Adjust the remaining stop

The plan may move the remaining stop toward the entry price, once its conditions are met. An entry-price stop aims to reduce nominal downside on the remainder, but fees and execution differences can still produce a loss.

Use planned partial booking

In the simplified four-point example, closing half the quantity at 204 realises four points on that half. If the remaining half later exits at exactly 196, the two halves offset before costs. This arithmetic does not apply unchanged to Type 1’s original 6.67-point stop.

Use the actual entry, stop, buffer and quantity in your calculation. For a long option, the planned entry-to-stop loss in rupees is approximately premium-point risk × total quantity, before costs; a worse fill can increase that loss.

Execution reference: Investor.gov: a stop’s execution price can differ from its trigger. Check the order types and trigger rules supported by your broker for the option contract.

Chapter 11 takeaway: Choose the entry style first. Type 1 anticipates a rebound with a width-based initial stop; Type 2 waits for Participation to be reclaimed and uses the observed low; Type 3 waits for a TCL cross and uses the nearby small swing low. Calculate the actual risk, define the risk-cover action, and then monitor Side Up 1 and Side Up 2.

PRACTICE CHECKPOINT

Write the plan before the trigger.

With P = 200 and O = 180, calculate Type 1’s initial stop and 1R price. For Type 2, compare a 196 reference low with an actual stop at 195.50. For Type 3, mark the TCL cross and nearby swing low on a chart. In each case, state the entry, stop, risk-cover action and Side Up targets.
