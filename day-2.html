Read the backdrop

Start with the market trend

A chart pattern has more meaning when you know what price was doing before it formed. The diagrams below show ideas, not historical prices.

Uptrend

Price tends to make higher highs and higher lows. A pause may become a continuation pattern.

Downtrend

Price tends to make lower highs and lower lows. A base may develop into a reversal pattern.

Sideways range

Price moves between approximate support and resistance until the range resolves.

Reversal or continuation? A reversal pattern may end a prior decline. A continuation pattern may pause an existing uptrend before it resumes. Neither outcome is certain; wait to see how price behaves at the relevant level.
Seven visual setups

Bullish chart patterns

Trace the price path, find the level being tested, and look for follow-through. Support and resistance are areas rather than exact prices.

Illustrative path · not market data
01 / Reversal · W formation

Double Bottom

Two lows form near the same support area. The intervening high marks the neckline.

What to observe

Watch for a sustained move above the neckline. The depth from support to neckline gives an illustrative measured move; a return below support weakens the setup.

Illustrative path · not market data
02 / Reversal · three support tests

Triple Bottom

Three troughs form around a common support zone with rallies between them.

What to observe

The resistance formed by the intervening highs is the neckline. A breakout may signal a change in trend; the neckline-to-low distance is a rough projection, not a price promise.

Illustrative path · not market data
03 / Reversal · left shoulder, head, right shoulder

Inverse Head & Shoulders

A deeper middle low (head) sits between two shallower lows (shoulders) after a decline.

What to observe

Connect the reaction highs to form the neckline. A break above it is the confirmation area; the head-to-neckline depth illustrates the measured move. Retests and failed breaks can occur.

Illustrative path · not market data
04 / Continuation · rising lows, flat highs

Ascending Triangle

Repeated highs press against a roughly flat resistance level while successive lows rise.

What to observe

A move through resistance can resolve the compression upward. The initial triangle height is sometimes projected from breakout, but price may also break down or stall.

Illustrative path · not market data
05 / Continuation · impulse and pullback

Bullish Flag

A sharp upward move forms a pole, then price pauses inside a short downward-sloping channel.

What to observe

A break above the flag channel suggests the prior trend may resume. The pole can be used as a visual measurement; a break below the channel challenges the continuation idea.

Illustrative path · not market data
06 / Reversal · curved base

Rounding Bottom

A decline gradually flattens into a broad base before price begins to climb again.

What to observe

The prior high area can serve as a neckline. A breakout may support a trend change; the base depth is only an illustrative distance. Formation takes time and may be uneven.

Illustrative path · not market data
07 / Continuation or reversal · rounded cup, smaller handle

Cup & Handle

A rounded recovery returns to a prior high, followed by a smaller pullback called the handle.

What to observe

Traders commonly watch the rim or handle resistance for a breakout. The cup depth offers a possible measured move, while a deep handle or failure under resistance reduces confidence.

Before acting on a chart

A simple reading checklist

01 · Prior trend

Is the formation appearing after a fall or during an existing rise?

02 · Key levels

Mark approximate support, resistance, neckline, or flag boundary.

03 · Break and retest

Observe whether price holds beyond the level or slips back through it.

04 · Risk

Plan what would invalidate the idea. Projected distances are illustrative, never guaranteed.
